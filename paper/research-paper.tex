\begin{abstract}
A sharper symmetric-sum comparison, obtained from a finite Bernoulli
mean--mode theorem, combines with elementary Chebyshev estimates to give an
explicit uniform argument for every $k\ge38001$. It also removes the
prime-counting premise from the existing range $49\le k\le38000$.
The argument uses no zeta-zero verification, no new prime enumeration, and
no enlarged finite certificate range. The already certified fixed gap and
twin-prime witnesses and the closed proofs for $k\le48$ are retained.
The elementary estimates, infinite tail, and exact all-$k$ assembly have
been implemented and compiled in Lean~4.34.0 without an analytic hypothesis.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Scope.}
This removes the additional analytic verification while retaining the original
fixed witnesses. This paper describes the current unconditional source in this repository.
The previous conditional submission \cite{submission} is superseded by the
new entry point \texttt{Solution.lean}.
No new prime or zero certificate is introduced.

\paragraph{Provenance and review.}
The density formula and initial cases follow Cambie \cite{cambie}; the

\paragraph{The density being classified.}
For a finite list $L$, let $F(L,r)$ be the coefficient of $z^r$ in
$\prod_{p\in L}((p-1)/p+z/p)$. The exact rational density at a prime $p$ is
$F(\{q:q<p,\ q\text{ prime}\},k-1)/p$.
Periodic counting proves that it is the natural density of positive integers
whose $k$th smallest distinct prime divisor is $p$.
The corresponding Lean theorem is
\texttt{hasNaturalDensity\_primeFactorDensity}.

\section{The coefficient comparison}
Let $p_1=2,p_2=3,\ldots$, $r=k-1$, and $g_i=p_{i+1}-p_i$.
For a finite prime prefix $\mathcal P$, write $e_j$ for the elementary
symmetric sum in weights $w_p=1/(p-1)$ and $R_r=e_{r-1}/e_r$.
The exact density identity in the existing proof is
\[
 d_{r+1}(p_{i+1})>d_{r+1}(p_i)
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 R_r(\{p:p<p_i,\ p\text{ prime}\})>g_i+1,
\]
when $e_r>0$. Reversing the strict inequality gives a descent.
An earlier strict descent and a later strict ascent exclude unimodality.
Put
\[
 A(x)=\sum_{p\le x}\frac1{p-1},\qquad
 B(x)=\sum_{p\le x}\frac{\log p}{p},\qquad E(x)=B(x)-\log x .
\]
Every prime sum displayed has the indicated finite support.

\begin{lemma}\label{mode}
Let $1\le r$ be an integer. For any finite prime prefix and $t>0$, put
$\mu(t)=\sum_p t/(p-1+t)$.
If $\mu(t)>r$, then $e_r>0$ and $R_r<t$.
If $e_r>0$, then $R_r\ge r/A$, where $A=\sum_p w_p$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Put $a_j=tw_{p_j}$ and let $c_m=e_m(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$, zero outside
$0\le m\le n$. Since every $a_j/(1+a_j)<1$, the hypothesis implies
$n>r$, so $c_r>0$.
Suppose $c_{r-1}\ge c_r$. Delete the $j$th variable and call the
resulting coefficients $b_m$. Newton's inequalities give
$b_{r-1}^2\ge b_{r-2}b_r$.
If $b_r>b_{r-1}$, then $b_{r-2}<b_{r-1}$ and
\[
 c_{r-1}=b_{r-1}+a_jb_{r-2}
       <b_r+a_jb_{r-1}=c_r,
\]
a contradiction. Thus $b_r\le b_{r-1}$ and
$c_r\le(1+a_j)b_{r-1}$. Summing
$a_jc_r/(1+a_j)\le a_jb_{r-1}$ gives
$\mu(t)c_r\le r c_r$, again a contradiction.
Consequently $c_{r-1}<c_r$, or $e_{r-1}<t e_r$.
For the lower comparison,
\[
 r e_r=\sum_{|S|=r-1}w_S\sum_{p\notin S}w_p\le A e_{r-1}.
\]
\end{proof}
The needed log-concavity and deletion comparison are proved in the Lean
implementation. Darroch's broader finite mean--mode theorem
\cite{darroch,gnedin} provides related context; the strict comparison above
is sufficient here.

\section{Elementary estimates and their domains}
We use the classical elementary Chebyshev inequalities, not an explicit
PNT or finite-height RH theorem. For $x\ge900$,
\begin{align}
 \psi(x)&\ge a x-\tfrac52\log x+1,\label{classlow}\\
 \vartheta(x)&\le\tfrac65a x-a\sqrt x+
 \frac5{4\log6}(\log x)^2+\tfrac52\log x,\label{classup}
\end{align}
where
$a=\log(2^{1/2}3^{1/3}5^{1/5}30^{-1/30})$ and
$0.92129\le a\le0.92130$; see \cite{cheb}, Section 1.1.
Also use the elementary bounds
$\vartheta(x)<(\log4)x$ and
$\psi(x)-\vartheta(x)\le2\sqrt x\log x$.

\begin{lemma}\label{theta}
One has
\[
 \vartheta(x)\le1.16x\ (x\ge1000),\qquad
 \vartheta(x)\le1.12x\ (x\ge2000),\qquad
 \vartheta(x)\ge0.4x\ (x\ge1000),
\]
and $\vartheta(x)\ge0.92x$ for $x\ge2^{30}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $Q(v)=0.7v^2+2.5v+2$. Equation \eqref{classup} gives
\[
 \vartheta(x)/x<1.10556-0.9212/\sqrt x+Q(\log x)/x.
\]
The function $Q(v)e^{-v}$ decreases for $v\ge\log1000$.
Thus dropping the negative term and using $\log1000<7$ gives
$1.10556+Q(7)/1000=1.15936<1.16$.

For $v\ge\log2000$, $Q(v)e^{-v/2}$ decreases: its derivative has
the sign of $-0.35v^2+0.15v+1.5<0$.
While $Q(\log x)/\sqrt x-0.9212$ is positive, multiplying it by
$1/\sqrt x$ preserves this decrease; afterwards the correction is
nonpositive. Hence its positive part is bounded by its value at $2000$.
Using $\log2000<7.61$ and $\sqrt{2000}<45$ gives
\[
 \vartheta(x)/x<
 1.10556+Q(7.61)/2000-0.9212/45<1.12.
\]
For the lower bounds combine \eqref{classlow} with
$\psi-\vartheta\le2\sqrt x\log x$.
At $1000$, use $\log1000<7$, $\sqrt{1000}>31$, and monotonicity of
$(\log x)/x$ and $(\log x)/\sqrt x$.
For $x\ge2^{30}$, the two loss terms decrease.
At the endpoint use $\log(2^{30})<21$ and $\sqrt{2^{30}}=32768$:
\[
 \frac{2.5(21)+1}{2^{30}}+\frac{42}{32768}<0.00129.
\]
Together with $a\ge0.92129$, this gives the lower bound $0.92x$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{E}
For every real $x\ge1000$, $-2<E(x)<1/5$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $n=\lfloor x\rfloor$.
Factorial identities and integral bounds give
\[
 B(n)\le\log(n!)/n+\vartheta(n)/n
 \le\log n+0.16+(1+\log n)/n<\log n+1/5.
\]
Here $(1+\log n)/n<8/1000$.
For a lower bound put $D=\sum_p\log p/[p(p-1)]$.
The six primes through $13$ contribute less than $0.7$, and
\[
 \sum_{m=14}^\infty\frac{\log m}{m(m-1)}
 \le\frac{\log14+1}{13}<0.28.
\]
The last inequality follows by telescoping summation by parts and
$\log(1+1/m)\le1/m$.
Thus $D<0.98$. Since $\log(n!)/n\le B(n)+D$ and
$\log(n!)/n\ge\log n-1$, $B(n)>\log n-1.98$.
Use $B(x)=B(n)$ and $\log x-\log n<1/n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{A}
For $x\ge1000$,
\[
                  A(x)<\log\log x+1.1+\frac4{\log x}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Elementary Mertens summation gives
\[
 \sum_{p\le x}\frac1p-\log\log x-M
 =\frac{E(x)}{\log x}-\int_x^\infty
                  \frac{E(u)}{u(\log u)^2}\,du .
\]
Lemma \ref{E} bounds its absolute value by $2.2/\log x$.
The elementary identity
$M=\gamma+\sum_p(\log(1-1/p)+1/p)$ identifies the constant
\cite{mertens}; no zeta-zero statement is involved.
The bound $\gamma\le H_{50}-\log50<0.59$, followed by the five primes
$2,3,5,7,11$, yields
\[
 M<0.59+\log(16/77)+2927/2310<0.29.
\]
For $C=\sum_p1/[p(p-1)]$, sum the nine primes through $23$.
For the rest, use
\[
 \sum_{m=24}^\infty\frac1{m(m-1)}
 \le\frac{24}{23}\sum_{m=24}^\infty\frac1{m^2}
 \le\frac{24}{529}.
\]
The resulting exact rational bound is $C<0.81$.
Since $A(x)\le\sum_{p\le x}1/p+C$, the constants sum to $1.1$.
Finally relax $2.2/\log x$ to $4/\log x$.
\end{proof}

\section{A smoothed lower bound retaining the small-variable contribution}
\begin{lemma}\label{smooth}
For $X\ge t\ge4752$,
\[
 \sum_{p\le X}\frac1{p+t}\ge
 \log\log X-\log\log t
 -\frac{\log(1+1000/t)}{\log t}
 -\frac{11/5}{\log t+2}. \tag{S}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Set $Y=1000$ and $f(u)=u/((u+t)\log u)$.
Summation by parts on the exact interval $(Y,X]$ gives
\[
 \sum_{Y<p\le X}\frac1{p+t}
 =\int_Y^X\frac{du}{(u+t)\log u}
 +f(X)E(X)-f(Y)E(Y)-\int_Y^X E(u)f'(u)\,du .
\]
The function $f$ increases and then decreases on $[Y,\infty)$:
$f'$ has the sign of $t(\log u-1)-u$, positive at $Y$ with exactly
one later zero. Write $F=\sup_{u\ge Y}f(u)$.
Using $-2<E<1/5$, split the last integral at the maximum.
The error is at least
$-(11/5)F+(1/5-E(Y))f(Y)\ge-(11/5)F$.
If the maximum is after $X$, replace $F$ in this calculation by $f(X)$.

For $z=t/u$, the inequality $\log(z\log u)\le z\log u-1$ and
$\log\log u>1$ show
$\log t+2\le(1+t/u)\log u$.
Therefore $F\le1/(\log t+2)$.
Finally,
\[
 \int_Y^t\frac{du}{(u+t)\log u}
 \ge\frac{\log(2t/(Y+t))}{\log t},\qquad
 \int_t^X\frac{du}{(u+t)\log u}
 \ge\log\log X-\log\log t-\frac{\log2}{\log t}.
\]
The $\log2$ contributions cancel. Omitted primes $p\le Y$ contribute
positively, proving (S).
\end{proof}
For a prefix containing every prime through $X$,
\[
             \mu(t)\ge t\sum_{p\le X}\frac1{p+t}. \tag{T}
\]
This follows from $p-1+t<p+t$. Together with Lemma \ref{mode},
(S) and (T) give a complete descent estimate.

\section{Symbolic primorial selection}
For real $r\ge38000$ define
\[
 v=\log r,\quad H=v-\log v+6/5,\quad
 \ell=\frac{r}{(11/10)H},\quad U=\log(5\ell),\quad
 t=\frac{25}{14}(\ell-U). \tag{P}
\]
For integer $r$, choose the first primorial $P$ with $\log P\ge\ell$.
Let $q^-<q$ be its largest two prime factors, and $L=\log P$.
This is an existence construction; no primorial or prime list is evaluated.

\begin{lemma}\label{selection}
One has $\ell\ge3670$, $U/\ell<0.0027$, $t>6535$, and
\[
          \ell\le L<\ell+U,\qquad 2q^->t.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The function $r/H(\log r)$ increases.
At $r=38000$, $H<9.4$, so $\ell\ge38000/(1.1\cdot9.4)>3670$.
Monotonicity of $\log(5x)/x$, with $\log18350<9.82$, gives
$U/\ell<0.0027$ and $t>(25/14)(3670-9.82)>6535$.

Minimality gives $\vartheta(q^-)<\ell$.
If $q^-\ge1000$, Lemma \ref{theta} gives $q^-<2.5\ell$;
otherwise this is automatic. Bertrand's theorem gives $q<2q^-<5\ell$.
Hence $\log q<U$ and $\ell\le L<\ell+U$.
Moreover $\vartheta(q^-)=L-\log q>\ell-U>3660$.
The rough upper bound $\vartheta(q^-)<1.4q^-$ implies $q^->2000$.
Apply $\vartheta(q^-)\le1.12q^-$ to obtain
$2q^->(25/14)(\ell-U)=t$.
\end{proof}

\section{The entire scalar ray, proved by differentiation}
\begin{lemma}\label{scalar}
For every real $r\ge38000$,
\[
 t\left(\log\ell-\log\log t
 -\frac{\log(1+1000/t)}{\log t}
 -\frac{11/5}{\log t+2}\right)>\frac{2001}{2000}r. \tag{D}
\]
With $M_*=\ell+\log(2000\ell)$, one also has
\[
 (1.09M_*+2)(\log M_*+1.1+4/M_*)<r. \tag{A}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $v_0=2109/200=10.545$. For $v\ge v_0$, $9<H<v$ and
$H/v\ge0.89$; the derivative of $H/v$ is
$(\log v-2.2)/v^2>0$, and its value at $v_0$ exceeds $0.89$.

By Lemma \ref{selection}, $\log t>8.78$ and $1000/t<0.154$.
Also $\log t<v-\log H+0.485$, since $\log(125/77)<0.485$.
Use $\log(1+s)\le s$, $\log1.1<0.096$, and
\[
 0.096+0.154/8.78+2.2/10.78<0.319.
\]
The bracket in (D) consequently exceeds
$B_0(v)=v-\log H-\log(v-\log H+0.485)-0.319$.
Put $J=v-\log H+0.485$.
Then $J>8$, $0<H'<1$, $H'/H<1/9$, and $0<J'<1$. Thus
\[
 (B_0-0.618H)'>1-1/9-1/8-0.618>0.
\]
At $v_0$, $9.3<H<9.39$, $2.23<\log H<2.24$, and
$\log J<\log8.8<2.175$, so
\[
 B_0(v_0)-0.618H(v_0)>
 10.545-2.24-2.175-0.319-0.618(9.39)=0.00798>0.
\]
Hence the bracket exceeds $0.618H$ on the whole infinite ray.
Since $t/r=(125/77)(1-U/\ell)/H$,
\[
 \frac{t}{r}(0.618H)>
 (125/77)(0.9973)(0.618)
 =1.000537987\ldots>2001/2000.
\]
This proves (D) by calculus, not by scanning integer parameters.

For (A), monotonicity of $\log(2000x)/x$ and
$\log7340000<15.81$ give $M_*<1.005\ell$.
Since $1.1H/v\ge0.979$ and $\log0.979>-0.022$,
\[
 \log\ell+1.1=H-0.1-\log(1.1H/v)<H-0.078.
\]
It follows that $\log M_*+1.1+4/M_*<H-0.078+0.005+4/3670<H$.
Also $1.09(1.005)+2/3670<1.1$, so $1.09M_*+2<1.1\ell$.
Their product is less than $1.1\ell H=r$.
\end{proof}

\section{The tail from k = 38001}
Use the CRT construction already present in the development.
Explicitly prescribe, for every prime $p\le q$,
\[
 a_p\equiv q^-\pmod p\ (p<q^-),\qquad
 a_{q^-}=q^--1,\qquad a_q=q^-+1 .
\]
Every $1\le m\le2q^--1$ belongs to one of these residues:
away from $q^--1,q^-,q^-+1$, use a prime divisor of $|m-q^-|$;
at those three exceptions use $q^-,2,q$, respectively.
Choose $0\le a<P$ satisfying $a\equiv-a_p\pmod p$.
Every $a+2P+m$ is composite, giving a block inside $(2P,4P)$
and a surrounding prime gap $g_-\ge2q^->t$.

The first integer of the block is $a+2P+1$ and is composite.
Bertrand's theorem puts its preceding prime above half this cutoff, hence
strictly above $P$. Thus the prefix at the descent contains every prime
through $P$. Equations (S), (T), (D) give $\mu(t)>r$ there.
Lemma \ref{mode} gives $R_r<t<g_-+1$.

Let $M=\log(400P)$ and let $N$ count primes in $(4P,400P]$.
Since $L\ge\ell\ge3670$, one has $P\ge2^{30}$.
The bounds in Lemma \ref{theta} give
\[
 NM\ge(0.92\cdot400-1.12\cdot4)P=363.52P.
\]
Also $M<P/5$: use $\log P\le0.007P$ for $P\ge1000$,
$\log400<6$, and $P\ge2^{30}$.
Put $G=\lceil1.09M\rceil$. Then
\[
 (N-1)G\ge1.09(N-1)M
 \ge396.2368P-1.09M>396P,
\]
because $0.2368\cdot5>1.09$.
The finite average-gap principle now supplies a globally consecutive gap
in this interval with
\[
 g_+<G<1.09M+1,\qquad g_++1<1.09M+2.
\]
Moreover $M=L+\log400<M_*$. The function
$\log s+1.1+4/s$ increases for $s>4$.
Lemma \ref{A} and (A) imply $(g_++1)A(400P)<r$, so
$R_r>g_++1$ at this later gap. Its coefficient denominator is positive:
the earlier mean bound already supplies more than $r$ primes.

The descent index is strictly below $\pi(4P)$, whereas the later gap
starts at an index at least $\pi(4P)$. This establishes the required
ordering even if the large gap's right endpoint is the first prime
above $4P$. Hence every integer $k\ge38001$ gives non-unimodality.

\section{The existing finite range, without a prime-counting premise}
The fixed certified gap supplies an earlier gap of length at least $4752$,
with preceding prime prefix containing every prime through
\[
 Y_*=\left\lfloor(\mathrm{recordGapCenter}-86399)/2\right\rfloor,
 \qquad \log\log Y_*>10.6.
\]
The existing later twin-prime certificate supplies a pair $Q,Q+2$ with
$\log(Q-1)>9$ and $\log\log(Q-1)<12.1$.
Their ordering and these size inequalities are already proved in the source.

Apply (S) with $X=Y_*$, $t=4752$.
Replace $\log(1+1000/t)$ by the larger $\log2<0.7$.
Since $8.46<\log4752<8.47$ and $\log8.47<2.137$,
\[
 \mu(4752)>
 4752(10.6-2.137-0.7/8.46-2.2/10.46)
 >4752(8.16)=38776.32>37999.
\]
Thus every $49\le k\le38000$, with $r=k-1\le37999$,
has a strict descent there by Lemma \ref{mode}.
For the later twin, Lemma \ref{A} gives
\[
             3A(Q-1)<3(12.1+1.1+4/9)<48\le r.
\]
The lower coefficient comparison forces a strict ascent across that twin.
The mean bound itself ensures enough primes for $e_r>0$; there is no
remaining count below $500000$ or other prime-count certificate to supply.

\begin{theorem}
Combine the existing closed proofs for $1\le k\le48$ and the existing
fixed gap and twin certificates with the preceding derivation.
For every positive integer $k$, the density sequence is unimodal exactly
when $k\le3$, with no Dusart or finite-height RH premise.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The domains are $1\le k\le48$, $49\le k\le38000$, and $k\ge38001$.
They are exhaustive and adjacent. The positive cases $1,2,3$ and the
remaining cases through $48$ are already proved in the existing source.
\end{proof}

\section{Computational and formal status}
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{p{0.43\linewidth}p{0.47\linewidth}}
\hline
Dependency & Treatment\\ \hline
Finite-height zeta zeros and completeness & Removed\\
Dusart error ray and sharp prime-counting bounds & Removed\\
New large finite verification interval & None\\
Original gap, twin primes, and cases through $48$ & Retained unchanged\\
Fixed scalar constants & Proved by rational inequalities and logarithmic tangents\\
New analytic estimates, tail, and all-$k$ theorem & Implemented and compiled in Lean~4.34.0\\ \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The final declaration is
\texttt{PrimeFactorUnimodality.}\allowbreak\texttt{completeClassification\_elementary}.
The entry point \texttt{Solution.lean} exports the same universal statement
as the Mathlib-only challenge. The key new declarations are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{smoothedPrimeSum\_lower};
\item \texttt{classification\_descent\_scalar};
\item \texttt{classification\_ascent\_scalar};
\item \texttt{elementary\_tail\_not\_isUnimodal}.
\end{itemize}

All fixed logarithmic constants in the implementation follow from the
certified bounds for $\log2,\log3,\log5$ and the two tangent inequalities
\[
 \log b+1-b/x\le\log x\le\log b+x/b-1\qquad(x,b>0).
\]
Here $b$ is an explicit rational product of powers of $2,3,5$.
The infinite scalar ray is proved by differentiation, with no scan of
integer parameters. There is no new prime or zero enumeration and no
implicit finite cutoff to verify. Reusing the byte-verified release objects
for the original fixed certificates affects build time only; the new
mathematical proof replaces the analytic verification premise.
