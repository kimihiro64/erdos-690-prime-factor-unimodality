\begin{abstract}
Let $p_1=2<p_2<\cdots$ be the primes, and let $d_k(p)$ denote the natural
density of positive integers whose $k$th smallest distinct prime divisor is
$p$. We formalize the classification asked for in Erd\H{o}s Problem 690:
the sequence $p\mapsto d_k(p)$ is unimodal exactly when $k\leq3$. The proof
starts from an exact finite recurrence, identifies it with natural density,
and converts adjacent differences into a comparison between a symmetric
polynomial ratio and a prime gap. Finite exact certificates, a certified
large prime gap, a certified twin-prime ascent, and a uniform Chinese-remainder
tail implement the Wang--Crapis strategy in Lean 4. The analytic inputs are
exposed in the exact real-variable, symmetric-error, and logarithm-cubed
short-interval forms used by the final all-$k$ assembly.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Erd\H{o}s Problem 690 concerns the position of a prime in the factorization of
a typical integer. If distinct prime divisors are listed increasingly, one
may ask whether the density of integers having a prescribed prime in position
$k$ rises to a mode and then decreases. The first three positions are
unimodal. The complete answer is negative from the fourth position onward:
every $k\geq4$ has a strict descent followed later by a strict ascent.

This repository is a Lean 4 formalization of the structural proof and its
all-$k$ assembly. Its central design choice is to make the density identity
and sign criterion explicit, so that finite computation is used only for
finite integer data and never as an unexamined oracle.

\section{Statement}
For $n\geq1$, write $q_1(n)<\cdots<q_{\omega(n)}(n)$ for its distinct prime
divisors, and define
\[
A_{k,p}=\{n\geq1:p\mid n,\ p\text{ prime, and }
\#\{q\mid n:q\text{ prime},q<p\}=k-1\}.
\]
Put $d_k(p)=\lim_{N\to\infty}|A_{k,p}\cap[1,N]|/N$.

\begin{definition}[Unimodality]
A sequence $a_i$ is unimodal if there is $m$ such that $a_i\leq a_{i+1}$
for $i<m$ and $a_{i+1}\leq a_i$ for $m\leq i$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Erd\H{o}s 690 classification]
For every $k\geq1$, the natural densities $d_k(p)$ exist and
\[
\bigl(i\mapsto d_k(p_i)\text{ is unimodal}\bigr)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
k\leq3. \tag{1}
\]
\end{theorem}

In Lean, the definitions are \texttt{primeFactorDensity}, \texttt{IsUnimodal}, and
\texttt{CompleteClassification} in \texttt{Definitions/KthPrimeFactor.lean}; the universal
conditional assembly is packaged in
\texttt{completeClassification\_of\_full\_record\_inputs}
in \texttt{Proof/CompleteClassification.lean}, and the public \texttt{Solution.lean} exposes
the same all-$k$ target. The cutoff-specific bounded-row theorem supplies the
finite-range analytic input to the final assembly.

\section{The exact finite density formula}
For a finite list $L$ and $r\geq0$, define
\[
\begin{aligned}
F([],0)&=1,&F([],r+1)&=0,\\
F((p,L),0)&=\frac{p-1}{p}F(L,0),\\
F((p,L),r+1)&=\frac{p-1}{p}F(L,r+1)+\frac1pF(L,r).
\end{aligned}\tag{2}
\]
If $L_p$ is the list of primes below $p$, then
\[
d_k(p)=\frac{F(L_p,k-1)}p. \tag{3}
\]
Divisibility by the finite set of primes below $p$ is periodic modulo their
product. Counting residue classes gives (2), while the factor $1/p$ imposes
divisibility by $p$. Thus the natural density exists without an infinite
Euler-product limit. The Lean declarations are \texttt{finiteDensity},
\texttt{prescribedFactorDensity}, and
\texttt{hasNaturalDensity\_isKthDistinctPrimeFactor\_eq\_prescribed}.

\section{First differences}
Let $p<q$ be consecutive primes. Expanding (2) at the next prime and clearing
positive denominators expresses the sign of $d_k(q)-d_k(p)$ in terms of the
gap $q-p$ and a ratio of adjacent elementary symmetric sums in the weights
\[
w_r=\frac1{p_r-1}\qquad(p_r<p).
\]
The exact criterion is `densityStep_iff_symmetricRatio_gap`; its reusable
upper and lower bounds are in `Helpers/SymmetricBounds`. A large gap forces a
descent, while a later sufficiently small consecutive-prime gap forces an
ascent. These two strict inequalities disprove unimodality.

\section{Proof architecture}
The formal proof has four layers.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Direct inequalities for the first three recurrence coefficients prove
  unimodality for $k=1,2,3$.
\item Generated exact certificates prove strict valleys for the initial finite
  ranges, with fixed-point weight sums extending the medium range.
\item A published record prime gap supplies a uniform finite-range descent;
  its factor and residue evidence is replayed by closed Lean arithmetic, and a
  closed record-twin certificate supplies the later ascent.
\item A Chinese-remainder construction gives a controlled composite block in
  the remaining tail. Explicit prime-counting, Chebyshev, and short-interval
  estimates select a later consecutive-prime pair, and the symmetric-ratio
  bounds give the strict ascent uniformly in $k$.
\end{enumerate}
The finite pieces are assembled by
`completeClassification_through48`,
`completeClassification_through38000_closed`, and the full record-range
modules. The final all-$k$ reduction is
`completeClassification_of_full_record_inputs`, which splits at the finite
cutoff, applies the published record-gap certificate through $7{,}430{,}000$,
and applies the uniform-tail theorem to every larger $k$. The final provider
adapter is `completeClassification_of_full_record_logCubed_inputs`: it accepts
real-variable prime-counting bounds, symmetric theta error bounds, and a
prime in an interval of relative width $1/\log^3 x$, and feeds them into the
exact universal theorem. The concrete finite-range interface is a bounded
list of verified prime rows, combined with the large-$x$ theta-error lemma by
`completeClassification_of_full_record_rows_at_large_cutoff`. The finite
cutoff and tail cutoff overlap, so the assembly has no uncovered boundary
case.

\section{Formal verification and certificates}
Definitions, density lemmas, first-difference lemmas, symmetric bounds,
certificates, and assembly theorems have separate Lean modules. Generated
prime prefixes and large integer certificates are ordinary Lean terms; their
numerical content is checked by kernel-replayed arithmetic, not trusted as
external numerical output.

The public `Challenge.lean`/`Solution.lean` pair is a narrow theorem boundary:
it exposes `CompleteClassification` itself, with no fixed-$k$ substitution and
no hidden range restriction. The internal all-$k$ reduction names its
remaining explicit analytic inputs rather than treating them as project
axioms.

\section{Source relationship and limitations}
The recurrence and initial ranges formalize Cambie's work. The large-range
organization adapts the complete-answer strategy of Wang and Crapis,
including the record-gap/twin-prime mechanism and uniform CRT tail. Dusart's
explicit estimates provide analytic background. This repository claims no
priority for those mathematical results and no independent expert review.

The analytic estimates are isolated in named interfaces so that each use is
visible in the proof architecture, including the explicit prime-counting
asymptotic reduction and the theta-error short-interval bridge. The public target is the universal
statement (1), whose quantifier ranges over every positive integer $k$; no
finite checkpoint is part of its specification. The finite and record-range
arguments and the uniform CRT tail are assembled at that universal boundary,
with the provider inputs kept explicit rather than hidden in a project axiom.
